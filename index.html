    Tools I Built

    What I built

    1. 01SoundDeckMy own sound library app, built to replace Soundly
    2. 02Rename & Sort WorkbenchOrganizes large numbers of sound files while I listen (inside SoundDeck)
    3. 03StepForgeTurns a few footstep samples into human, beast or monster steps that follow the gait
    4. 04Creature Sound BuilderBuilds creature sounds by layering sources (inside SoundDeck)
    5. 05TubeVaultA study app that keeps YouTube videos with subtitles, descriptions and summaries

    What I learned building them with AI

    All five started from a real point where my work got stuck. I'm not a developer, so I decide what is inconvenient and how it should work, and I write the code together with AI (Claude). When I find something to fix while using them, I fix it right away.

    Building them made me put into words what I used to do by feel: where a footstep lands, how creature layers stack, how a library should be named. Because of that, I got better at breaking work into steps, managing many files without mistakes and learning new tools quickly.

    Sound design document

    The Last of Us Part II · Sound Design Document

    Foley recording · distance-based breathing trigger · voice direction for Ellie

    Project

    • Game sound design built around my own Foley recordings
    • Immersion that feels like real gameplay

    Situation

    Ellie escapes from enemies and starts fighting back inside a building.

    • Flow: stealth → chase → melee
    • A fight for survival against attacking enemies

    Ambience (AMB)

    Outside

    Weather
    Foggy dawn turning into morning
    Tone
    Cold, damp air and limited sight

    Inside

    Space
    A reverberant building interior
    Mood keywords
    Horror / Cinematic / Scary
    I used the room's reverb and low-frequency ambience so the tension never easily resolves.

    To build tension and heavy air, I shaped the mood with Foley sounds.

    • Wind through a danso (Korean bamboo flute)
    • Air escaping from a balloon
    • Scratches on guitar strings

    Character sound

    Ellie

    Personality
    Bold and brave
    Voice
    Female, 17 to 19 / mid-range / anger mixed with tension
    Foley
    Backpack, shirt, jeans, regular sneakers
    Weapons
    Bow & arrows (normal / explosive), machete

    Enemies

    Types
    Female / strong male / thick, powerful male voice
    Foley
    Jacket, rain suit, bag, shotgun Foley
    Weapons
    Bow & arrows, hammer (heavy weapon)

    UI sound concept

    Instead of traditional UI sounds, the UI uses the real movement of objects.

    Objects
    Gear / can / trash / Foley objects

    Crafting UI sketch

    • Changing a light bulb
    • Wrapping something in cloth
    • A low-pitched can moving
    • Twigs rubbing together
    By combining real objects, I made crafting feel tactile, as if you could hold it in your hands.

    Distance-based trigger system

    Ellie's breathing changes step by step as the distance to the enemy changes.

    • Danger reads without a visual HUD
    • It works directly on the player's state of mind
    5 m or more
    No breathing · a relatively safe zone
    2 to 3 m
    Light, short breaths · on guard
    Within 1 m
    Rough, heavy breathing · extreme pressure right before being spotted
    Within 1 m especially, I made the breathing deliberately rough to say "if you move now, you'll be caught" through sound.

    Sound palette (recorder: PCM-A10)

    Bow loading & arrow

    • Twisting a paper-wrapped wooden chopstick for bowstring tension
    • Rubbing processed chopsticks together for the draw

    Footsteps on grass

    • Coins in a bag for the texture of stepping on grass
    • EQ cut from 1k to 20k to remove the metallic tone
    • Pitch -1 and reverb to match the picture

    Voice direction for Ellie

    Why I cast a voice actor myself

    The main goal was to give her real character and the strongest possible immersion.

    In The Last of Us Part II especially, I tried to build tension through her breathing, small sounds of presence and lines.

    Casting brief

    Age
    Around 17 to 19
    Gender
    Female
    • A rough, fighter-like character
    • Injured and moving secretly out of the enemies' sight
    • Under extreme tension and stress
    • Whispering quietly outside of combat, and a strong, intense voice for rough movement in combat

    Breathing trigger

    The closer she gets to the enemy, the rougher her breathing. While moving, I also added breathing now and then. (The distance stages are the same as the trigger system above.)

    Direction by scene

    This is the direction I gave the voice actor for each scene. I left room for her to adjust it in a similar feel. Click a time to play the video from that moment.

    1. Pulling out an embedded arrow. The pain builds right up to the pull (rising groan… then a sharp gasp!) → after the pull, about 2 seconds of rough breathing with the pain still there
    2. "I have to be careful not to get caught…" (in a careful voice)
    3. Straining to pull out an arrow, with the mouth closed: hnn… ha!
    4. "This should do it…"
    5. "Damn it…"
    6. Catching her breath and setting off: a big breath in through the nose, then a short "hoo!" out as the fight begins
    7. Rough breathing while running, and a furious "hraah!!" when bringing the blade down
    8. A startled voice while dodging
    9. Ellie's attack voices: hit 1 "ha!" / hit 2 a little softer "hh..!" / hit 3 straining as the weapons clash and scrape "nnngh…!!"
    10. Being choked: "khh!"
    11. "Khh…!" Groaning when thrown to the floor (1:25 to 1:30 like the reference voice, or even bigger)
    12. A surprised voice, like a sharp inhale: "huh!"
    13. Running: moving with rough breathing
    14. Teeth clenched, "nngh!! aah!" for the movement → at 1:39, striking the enemy: "Die!!!"
    15. Picking up arrows: rough, exhausted breathing
    16. Getting hit: "ugh…!" in pain
    17. Three bow shots, emotion rising each time: shot 1 "Die..!" / shot 2 "Please…" / shot 3 "I said die!" (fully peaked) → then walking on, breathing out, exhausted
    18. About to strike the enemy, full of anger: "Shut up!!"
    19. Bringing the blade down: "hraah!!"
    20. Until the outro: panicked breathing with a slight cold shiver

    Design intent

    • This sound design uses sound both to carry information and to work on the player's mind.
    • With distance-based breathing, Foley from real objects and an ambience that keeps the sense of space, the player can feel the situation without even looking at the screen.

    Closing

    In the end, the goal of this project was to bring the urgent, overwhelming combat of the original to life through sound, while approaching it like a film scene built around voice and sound.

    I aimed to blur the line between game and film by combining real gameplay sound with cinematic voice direction. The key was to make the viewer feel like they were playing the game, not just watching a scene.

    This piece shows how I see sound: as part of the game system and as a storytelling tool. Thank you for listening.
